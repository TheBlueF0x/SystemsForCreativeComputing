FLOSS (Free Libre and Open Source Software) is software that is owned by the users. It gives users the ability to customise, add to, use and distribute the software. As expected by the name is it also free, so there are no costs involved, meaning it is far more accessible to a mainstream audience. Open source software often has many contributors, for example with Linux, where almost 17,000 people have contributed to its kernel alone. This means that the public has far more control over the source code, and can not only see exactly how it works, but can also contribute to it in a controlled way that the original makers of the software can control, so not just anybody can add anything. The idea of it being free means that the ethics behind it is that it is not made or controlled by a corporation (although some can be made by them just not controlled), meaning that there is not a profit incentive, and it can be made for a specific use case without needing to worry about breaking even, making profit, or being controlled by shareholders. Because of this, many people choose FLOSS software over others as it can be seen as more ethical, and can be much more easily obtained due to it being free.
